# Field-tested reply patterns

Load this file BEFORE drafting any agency reply or follow-up. These are
techniques from live work, not one-off stories. Combine with the last-letter
check in `shared/ground-truth-standard.md`.

## When to invoke

- Agency closed as "no responsive records" / "request closed"
- Agency cited a regulation, fee, or form from a different agency
- Clerk or records officer pointed at a webpage instead of producing or certifying
- Custodian A says they lack the file because filer B never submitted it
- 311 / queue / operational notes are the only written standard
- About to send a follow-up on a thread another agent may already have answered
- Tempted to put a case/ticket/FOIA number in a letter that you do not have

## 1. Zero-records close is a factual close, not a withholding

A "no responsive records / we consider this closed" letter is a claim that
they do not possess the records after a search. It is not an exemption.

Do:

- Demand a written search certification: who searched, which mailboxes and
  shared drives, date range, and the actual terms used.
- File a standalone § 67.21(c) EFNQ for each original item (existence /
  quantity / form / nature / custodian / date range), explicitly not a (b)
  production request. 7-day clock. Load `efnq-and-two-sides-pattern.md`.
- If they are not the custodian, require a § 67.21 referral to the office
  that is.
- File the same items on the other side of the conversation (DEM/HSOC, DPH
  Street Team, HSH, SFPD, Supervisor). A Mayor close is not citywide
  non-existence.
- SOTF only after `~/tools/nr-state --gate` AND they blow the EFNQ 7-day or
  repeat the bare close.

Do not:

- Lead with § 67.26 / § 67.27. Nothing was withheld.
- Lead with § 67.34.
- Accept a three-line close as a search.

Operational notes (HSOC Queue, 311 closure text) that apply a standard
("4 ft," "ADA compliant," "able to remain") ARE the records. Ask for the
Mayor-side or department-side written direction behind those notes, or a
written "no current policy" plus who holds it now. A dead injunction is not
current law; continued use after the order ended is a policy choice.

## 2. Wrong-cite challenge

If they attach a fee, deadline, or procedure to another agency's regulation
(example: VA FOIA citing NASA 14 CFR), do not pay and do not argue the
wrong title. Quote the cite they used, quote the cite that actually binds
them (verify both live), and demand they withdraw the misplaced rule.

## 3. Produce or certify — do not accept a webpage

A clerk or records officer who points at a help page or a lawyer-referral
line has not searched. Judiciary Law § 255 and similar clerk-search statutes
require them to copy the existing file or certify that it cannot be found.
Do not ask for legal advice or a new manual. Ask for the existing paper or
the certificate.

## 4. Gap goes to the actual filer

When custodian A certifies they do not have X because filer B never filed
it, stop pounding A. Take the gap to B and to the program that should have
noticed the miss. Do not ask A to restate the certification (last-letter
check).

## 5. Sent-folder check before any follow-up

Before sending, search Gmail Sent on that exact thread. Chief of Staff and
other agents send accountability drafts. Do not stack a second letter on the same
thread unless the user explicitly wants the second one.

## 6. Never invent identifiers

Do not put a 311 / FOIA / FOIL / NextRequest / court number in a letter
unless you have the real one from a primary system. A third-party portal
ref (ImproveBayArea verint/ref) is not the city's public 10-digit case ID.
Send without the number, or wait. Never fabricate.

## 7. Standing records-letter boilerplate

Every records request or reply that still asks for production includes:

- rolling production
- digital files only of existing files or data (not new reports)
- Google Drive upload as an ADA Title II modification
- free or fee-waived
- ID only after a written ask

Never brand GOV on anything that leaves the house.

## 8. Time the channel, not the bot

Faxes and other hours-restricted channels use the receiver's local hours
(destination ZIP / published window), not Pacific time. After-hours items
go on the fax-business-hours queue. Never FaxDrop. Never send unapproved
items.
