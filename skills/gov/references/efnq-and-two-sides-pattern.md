# SF Sunshine § 67.21(c) EFNQ + Two-Sides-of-the-Conversation Parallel Filing

Two related patterns for breaking blanket attorney-client privilege defenses
in SF public-records work. Captured 2026-05-26 from the SOTF EOTC hearing on
File 26073 (Sanders v. SF City Attorney).

## When to invoke this reference

- A SF agency (any department, but especially the City Attorney's office)
  has invoked attorney-client privilege or work-product privilege to
  withhold records under SF Admin Code § 67.21(b) or the CPRA
- The complainant filed a § 67.21(b) records request and got a blanket
  privilege response with no description of what is being withheld
- A SOTF panel has ruled "no violation" on a § 67.21(b) complaint because
  the agency's response cited categorical privilege
- Two-party communications are at issue (legislative drafting,
  agency-to-agency coordination) — meaning the SAME records exist on both
  sides of a conversation, but only one side can assert privilege

## Pattern 1 — § 67.21(c) EFNQ Request

SF Admin Code § 67.21(c) creates a duty that goes BEYOND state CPRA and
operates ABOVE the privilege regime. It is the most under-used provision
in the Sunshine Ordinance.

### Verbatim statutory text

> "A custodian of a public record shall assist a requester in identifying
>  the existence form and nature of any records or information maintained
>  by, available to, or in the custody of the custodian, **whether or not
>  the contents of those records are exempt from disclosure**, and shall,
>  when requested to do so, provide in writing within **seven days**
>  following receipt of a request, a statement as to the **existence,
>  quantity, form, and nature** of records relating to a particular subject
>  or questions with enough specificity to enable a requester to identify
>  records in order to make a request under subsection (b)."

Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_admin/0-0-0-19673

### Why this defeats blanket-privilege defenses

- The duty applies **whether or not** the records are exempt — privilege
  does not extinguish it.
- Haynie v. Superior Court (2001) 26 Cal.4th 1061 — the City Attorney's
  go-to citation — addresses only the search/production duty under state
  CPRA. It does not address § 67.21(c).
- § 67.21(c) was authored to require an EFNQ description that does NOT
  break privilege but DOES tell the requester what universe of records
  exists. This was confirmed on the record by SOTF EOTC Member David
  Pilpel at the May 26, 2026 hearing on File 26073:
   - Pilpel said he personally helped write § 67.21(c)
   - "This is a specific, special provision in the Sunshine Ordinance
     that is not in state law, that is beyond what's required in state law"
   - "Written carefully so as to not break any privilege, but to indicate
     ... during this period of time, somebody was drafting legislation"
- 7-day clock is independent of § 67.21(b)'s 10-day clock.

### Template structure for an EFNQ request

1. **Subject line**: must include "§ 67.21(c) EFNQ request" or
   "existence/form/nature/quantity request" — magic words matter; the
   panel in File 26073 ruled that without this framing the agency's
   § 67.21(b) response was sufficient
2. **Quote § 67.21(c) verbatim** with operative phrases emphasized
3. **State explicitly that this is NOT a (b) request** — it is a separate
   request under a separate statutory provision with a separate timeline
4. **For each subject area, request the 6 elements**:
   - EXISTENCE — yes / no / cannot ascertain without search
   - QUANTITY — exact count or range ("fewer than 10," "10–50," "more
     than 50")
   - FORM — email, draft, PDF, calendar entry, IM, voicemail
   - NATURE — general topic description phrased not to reveal privileged
     content
   - CUSTODIAN — which office/unit holds the records
   - DATE RANGE — narrowest accurate date range within the requested
     window
5. **State explicitly what the request is NOT**: not seeking production,
   not seeking privileged content, not a Vaughn index
6. **State the 7-day deadline** with the exact calculated date

### Citation framework

Always include in any EFNQ request:

- SF Admin Code § 67.21(c) — operative duty
- SF Admin Code § 67.27 — every withholding cites specific authority
- SF Admin Code § 67.26 — withholding kept to minimum
- SF Admin Code § 67.1(b) — broad construction in favor of disclosure
- SF Admin Code § 67.28(b) — public-interest fee waiver
- Cal. Gov. Code § 7920.545 — definition of "writing"
- Cal. Evidence Code § 953 — privilege belongs to client, can be waived
  by client (relevant when filing the client-side companion request)

## Pattern 2 — Two-Sides-of-the-Conversation Parallel Filing

When the records at issue are communications between two government bodies
(or a government body and an outside party), the SAME records exist on
BOTH sides. The privilege analysis differs by side.

### The asymmetry

| Side | Custodian | Privilege analysis |
|------|-----------|--------------------|
| Attorney side | City Attorney's office | Asserts attorney-client + work-product. Strong defense per Roberts v. City of Palmdale (1993) 5 Cal.4th 363. |
| Client side | Supervisor's office, dept head, commission | Holds same records on the client side. **Client controls the privilege** (Cal. Evidence Code § 953) and can voluntarily disclose or waive. |
| Third party | Outside lobbyist, developer, constituent | If they hold copies, privilege is typically waived by virtue of disclosure to a third party (Cal. Evidence Code § 952). |

### The rule

**File parallel requests on each side of the conversation, as separate
records requests, framed for the privilege regime that applies to that
side.**

- Don't file one omnibus request — it lets the most privilege-protected
  party block the whole thing.
- Don't file the same request to all parties — each request should be
  optimized for the recipient's privilege posture.
- Cross-reference the requests in each, so the agencies know that
  refusing one will be visible to the other and to SOTF.

### Why the client-side request often produces more

1. **The client controls the privilege.** Many Supervisors, department
   heads, and policy makers are willing to disclose communications
   voluntarily — especially if the public-interest balance is clear.
2. **Calendar entries and transmittal records** typically are NOT
   privileged on either side, but agencies sometimes hide them
   inside an attorney-client privilege bundle. The client-side
   request surfaces them directly.
3. **Constituent communications, lobbyist communications, internal
   talking points, fiscal sheets** on the client side were never
   privileged to begin with.
4. **Policy origin records** — who requested the legislation, when,
   why — sit with the sponsor's office, not the drafter's office.

### Template structure for the client-side companion request

1. Direct to the client custodian (e.g., Supervisor's office), with
   appropriate Cc to Clerk of the Board for NextRequest routing
2. State in the opening that this is separate from a companion request
   to [the attorney custodian] covering substantially the same subject
3. Explain the privilege-asymmetry rationale briefly — that the client
   controls the privilege and can voluntarily disclose
4. Request the records under § 67.21(b), not (c) — the client side
   typically doesn't need the EFNQ end-run; the records aren't
   categorically privileged
5. Enumerate the records with these typical buckets:
   - External / constituent communications
   - Internal office work product (NOT prepared by attorneys)
   - Communications with the attorney custodian (with privilege caveat)
   - Calendar entries and meeting notes
   - Lobbying / stakeholder records
   - Records of the policy origin
6. Standard SF Sunshine boilerplate (10-day deadline, fee waiver, § 67.27
   specificity for any withholdings)

## When to escalate

If the EFNQ response is also deficient (e.g., agency repeats blanket
privilege with no EFNQ statement), the next levers are:

1. **Supervisor of Records petition under SF Admin Code § 67.21(d)** —
   parallel to the SOTF complaint, can move faster on technical
   non-compliance
2. **Second SOTF complaint under § 67.21(c)** — distinct from the (b)
   complaint, on the new 7-day clock
3. **Brown Act § 54960.1 cure-and-correct** — if the records relate to
   an action taken without proper notice (e.g., a vote, an appointment,
   a removal). 90-day clock from the action.

## Reference incident

**2026-05-26 — SOTF EOTC File 26073 (Sanders v. SF City Attorney):**
SOTF panel ruled 3-0 no violation on a § 67.21(b) complaint because the
City Attorney's office responded within 10 days and cited attorney-client
and work-product privilege as the categorical basis for withholding.
Member Pilpel introduced § 67.21(c) on the record, distinguished it from
the agency's caselaw (most of which arises in litigation context, not
legislative drafting), and noted that the requester had not invoked
§ 67.21(c) in the underlying request.

The lesson: when the subject matter is legislative drafting or
agency-to-agency coordination — where the privilege analysis is
weakest and the client-side records are richest — file BOTH:

1. A § 67.21(c) EFNQ request to the attorney custodian, on the 7-day
   clock, designed to extract the description without breaking
   privilege.
2. A § 67.21(b) records request to the client custodian, on the 10-day
   clock, designed to extract the client-side records directly.

This sequence was articulated on the record by Pilpel as the proper
procedural path; the SOTF ruling provides the precedent that the
attorney-side records will not come out through (b) alone.
